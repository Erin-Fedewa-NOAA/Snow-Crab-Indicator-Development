#INDICATOR_NAME
"Annual_Snow_Crab_Male_Size_Terminal_Molt_Model"

#DESCRIPTION
"Male size at 50% maturity (carapace width, in mm) is derived from abundance-weighted maturity-at-size ogives and calculated as the size at which the probability mature first crosses 50% each year. Maturity ogives are estimated from spatiotemporal generalized linear mixed effects models in sdmTMB for newshell males only.  Proposed sign of the relationship is positive."

#STATUS_TRENDS
"The size at which 50% of the male snow crab population underwent terminal molt increased by ~4 mm to an estimate of 81.14mm carapace width from 2025 to 2026. Although annual size at maturity is highly variable, this indicator has been trending down for the past three decades."

#FACTORS
"Male size at maturity is primarily driven by cohort size, which negatively impacts size at maturity, and the abundance of large males, which has a positive effect due to mate competition (Murphy 2021; Mullowney and Baker, 2021, Ryznar et al., in review)."

#IMPLICATIONS
"The potential for sperm limitation in populations depleted of large mature male snow crab may decrease reproductive potential of the stock (Baker et al., 2022). In addition, declining size at maturity creates a negative feedback loop where high exploitation rates on large males reduces their abundance, which in turn alters the maturity schedules of the remaining population, resulting in less large males and a decline in the stock's overall reproductive potential (Ryznar et al., in review)."
